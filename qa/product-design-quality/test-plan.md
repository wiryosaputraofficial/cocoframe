## QA decisions

- **Which environments must this feature pass in?** Local development, production-like build, and isolated npm package-smoke projects.
- **Which browsers, devices, and viewport ranges are required?** Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit desktop; 320, 390, 768, 1366, and 3840 pixel viewports; keyboard-only interaction, reduced motion, and 200 percent text zoom.
- **Which fixtures, accounts, roles, and data states are safe to use?** Use examples/basic, the approved CocoSpec, the approved Design Profile, generated documentation, and isolated temporary workspaces. No accounts, secrets, external services, or production data. Temporary fixtures must be cleaned after use.
- **Which failures must block release, and which results may be explicitly waived?** Any required gate failure, critical or high defect, WCAG contrast failure, destructive overflow or clipping, unapproved missing component, Design Profile hash conflict, or sensitive evidence blocks release. Low or medium design deviations require explicit Product Owner or QA Reviewer acceptance; AI cannot approve.
- **Which accessibility standard and assistive interactions must be verified?** WCAG 2.2 AA keyboard-only operation logical focus order visible focus contrast semantic labels announcements manual screen-reader review 200 percent text zoom forced colors reduced motion and ergonomic non-overlapping touch targets.
- **Which security, authorization, privacy, and abuse cases require negative testing?** Negatively test unknown and unsafe CSS tokens, URL script or expression injection, malformed profiles, workspace traversal and linked escapes, unsanitized evidence, secret exposure, stale approvals, and unauthorized mutation. Read-only audits must not mutate state, and an AI agent cannot approve its own result.
- **Which measurable performance thresholds must pass?** Parse and validate a normal Design Profile within 100 milliseconds. Complete a static audit within 2 seconds for up to 500 components or 10000 token usages. Each viewport gate is limited to 30 seconds and the complete visual audit to 2 minutes. Profiles are limited to 256 kilobytes and evidence to 10 files of 10 megabytes each. Theme adds no island or hydration payload.
- **Which migrations, integrations, or legacy behaviors require regression coverage?** The feature is additive and opt-in. Projects without a Design Profile retain existing visuals. Older CocoQA records remain readable. Migration is explicit and never automatic. Theme remains server-first. Agent contracts remain provider-independent. Rollback removes profile adoption and regenerates derived artifacts from canonical sources.
- **Which areas need exploratory testing beyond scripted acceptance cases?** Run a 30 minute risk-focused exploration covering profile edge cases, nested Theme overrides, dark and forced-colors modes, long content, 200 percent zoom, stale hashes, component reuse, icon labeling, and cancellation. Keep only sanitized notes and declared screenshots.
- **Which approved Design Profile, theme states, and component inventory are in scope?** Use Design Profile cocoframe-foundation at examples/basic/cocoframe.design.json with light and dark themes and SHA-256 6c084176b74b974da3b8e21c3fa307ea80beefc83cd2829fbb715e3ec05022bb. Inventory includes all project.inspect application components, @cocoframe/ui primitives, and @cocoframe/icons linear. No component exceptions are approved.
- **Which measurements and manual reviews must prove each Product Design Quality principle?** Use unit evidence for profile parsing, token safety, contrast, and diagnostics; rendered HTML evidence for server-first Theme behavior; inspect and source evidence for component reuse and icon origin; Playwright computed metrics for spacing, color, radius, responsive reflow, overflow, keyboard, images, and client errors; manual review for screen readers, forced colors, zoom, and visual hierarchy. Fidelity is not applicable without a completed CocoRef.
- **Which exact viewport, zoom, theme, and interaction states require visual verification?** Verify 320 by 568, 390 by 844, 768 by 1024, 1366 by 768, and 3840 by 2160 viewports; light and dark themes; keyboard focus, hover, disabled and error states; reduced motion, forced colors, long content, and 200 percent text zoom.
- **Who may accept low- or medium-severity design deviations, and which deviations always block release?** Low- and medium-severity design deviations may be accepted only through explicit joint approval by the Product Owner or Designer and the QA or Release Reviewer, with rationale, affected targets, follow-up, and expiry recorded. AI agents and implementation authors cannot approve their own results. Critical or high defects are never waivable. WCAG contrast, focus, or accessibility failures; destructive overflow, clipping, or overlapping controls; missing or unapproved components or CocoRef decisions; unsafe or unknown tokens; sensitive evidence; stale Design Profile, reference, or component hashes; and any required gate failure always block release.
